Naval Action Automation handbook

Use the one-minute start, then open only the task you need. Every current tool is covered below.

Start in one minute

  1. Check the market day and update time on the Dashboard.
  2. Open the task you need below; every interactive tool shows when a result is incomplete.
  3. Before acting, verify important prices, stock, and quantities in the game.

Example: Start with one small check

You want to plan a purchase before sailing.

  1. Check the Dashboard market day and update time.
  2. Open Current market and search for the item you need.
  3. Confirm price, stock, and quantity in game before acting.

What you get: You have a dated planning reference and a clear in-game check to make.

Find an item, price, or market changeSearch the public Naval Action database of current NPC port prices or compare saved market days.

Example: Buy 100 Oak Logs

You need 100 Oak Logs and want to compare the current shop evidence.

  1. Open Current market and search for Oak Logs.
  2. Review the listed unit price, sale stock, update time, and port tax rate; make sure the listing shows at least 100 in stock.
  3. Choose a candidate port, then verify the price and stock in game before buying.

What you get: You have a current shop comparison for a 100-log purchase.

  1. Use Dashboard for each item’s cheapest port with stock. Use Current market when you need every matching offer.
  2. In Current market, search or filter and then sort the results. ‘Listed unit price (before tax)’ is the raw shop listing; the matching port tax is shown separately. ‘Port shop pays you’ remains the separate sell direction.
  3. Use Today to compare the latest saved market day with the previous one. In History, select an item to see its daily lowest, median, and highest listed unit prices before transaction tax.
  4. Download price list exports the current contract-free NPC offers as CSV. Missing values stay missing, and player contracts are excluded everywhere.

A market day starts after 10:00 UTC maintenance. The first sync is attempted at 10:30 UTC and retried every 15 minutes until a validated update is published.

Use the interactive map for ports, suppliers, and routesOpen port ledgers, compare up to three pinned ports, and chart no more than two experimental routes.

Example: Compare two possible ports

Two ports may serve your next pickup, but you do not know which route is easier.

  1. Open Caribbean Map and pin both ports.
  2. Use Compare to view them together, then set one as start and the other as destination.
  3. Inspect the plotted line and port details, and confirm access and water in game.

What you get: You can compare the two ports and inspect a proposed route between them.

  1. Right-click a port, or choose the visible Port actions button after selecting one, for Set as start, Add as stop, Set as destination, Remove from route and one-step Undo. The menu also opens Navigator, the ledger, suppliers and the opt-in warehouse or note tools without changing inventory by itself.
  2. Navigator accepts at least three readings to the same ship position from the in-game Trader Tool. For example, 65k means 65,000 map units. You can review OCR from a local screenshot, correct uncertain ports or distances, or enter a manual correction. The result is an uncertainty area and timestamp, not exact GPS; a route bearing may follow the next port or a chart bend.
  3. Estimated travel time is marked Experimental (WIP). It is available only for listed Galeon fleet and cargo profiles and starts from a straight-line recorded-trip baseline; it does not prove detour time or independent accuracy. An optional timer compares a frozen prediction with elapsed time and can be exported deliberately.
  4. Port notes remain private browser data and are included only when you deliberately create an offline backup. Warehouse actions first require explicit profile and warehouse selection; opening a map action never reads or changes inventory silently.
  5. Open Caribbean Map from the main navigation. Map V5 is now the current map at the canonical atlas address; an old atlas-v4 bookmark opens the same map.
  6. Click a port to open its information panel without moving or recentering the chart. Choose Zoom to port only when you want the map to move.
  7. The Port tab shows port facts and the Latest market update. Search the compact Port market, then expand For sale or Bought or consumed here for NPC price and stock evidence.
  8. Use Suppliers to choose an item and filter by nation, region, depth, stock, starting port, comparison distance, or sort order. Every match gets an ink ring; the five lowest observed prices get numbered seals. The results list keeps price and stock readable, and selecting a result opens that port.
  9. A sale recorded as 0 means zero at the latest update, not guaranteed zero when you arrive; stock can replenish. Stock not reported is unknown and is never treated as zero.
  10. Pin a port before opening another if you want to retain it for comparison. Pin at most three ports; Compare works with two or three. Without pins, selecting another port replaces the open port window.
  11. Under Route actions, assign Set as start, Add as stop, and Set as destination in any order. Use Remove from route to correct the draft; the plotted sequence still runs from its chosen start through stops to its destination.
  12. The first plotted route becomes Route A, the baseline. A second becomes Route B. If a third route arrives, choose Replace Route B or Keep current routes; Route A is not silently replaced.
  13. Supply & Cargo and Trade Planner can open a dedicated map tab with Show on Map V5. Crafting Projects reaches the same handoff through Open supplies in shopping map. Warehouse Manager has no direct map handoff: open Supply & Cargo, explicitly opt in to a saved warehouse profile, review warehouse pickups and remaining NPC purchases, then choose Show on Map V5.
  14. Route markers use a compass for the start, a crate for an NPC purchase, a chest for a warehouse pickup, both symbols when both actions occur at one port, a plain circle for another stop, and a flag for the destination. The A or B label and number identify the route and stop order.
  15. When automatic routing is unresolved, its port markers remain visible and the route card warns that no safe line or order was established. Do not interpret missing route lines as a navigable passage.
  16. Automatic charting only attempts to avoid land in the current game-derived mask. It does not prove safe water, depth for your ship, a harbour approach, wind or boost conditions, travel time, or ETA. Distances are relative game-coordinate comparisons; confirm important journeys in game.
  17. Read Latest market update before acting. Market evidence is a daily post-maintenance snapshot, not a live feed, so prices and stock may change after publication.

Routes and handoffs remain browser-local planning aids. Verify port access, prices, stock, quantities, and the sailing path in Naval Action.

Save and maintain warehouse stockEnter stock manually, import Owned values from screenshots, and review combined totals.

Example: Record 250 logs, then remove 50

You want your private warehouse notes to reflect stock used in game.

  1. Open Warehouse Manager, choose a profile, port and Oak Logs, then use Set actual quantity to enter 250 and review the result before saving.
  2. After using 50 in game, choose Remove used stock and enter 50; check that the preview shows 250 → 200 before saving.
  3. Open Recent changes to review both saved corrections.

What you get: Your browser-local warehouse profile records the exact change from 250 to 200 Oak Logs for that port.

  1. Create or select a profile. For manual entry, choose a warehouse port, item, and quantity, then select Save item. Saving quantity 0 removes that item from that warehouse.
  2. Open one saved warehouse or All warehouses. In the combined view, search or sort, expand or collapse categories and items, and use Quick Edit to jump back to the exact manual-entry row.
  3. For screenshot import, first select the destination profile. Add 1–10 complete PNG, JPEG, or WebP shop screenshots with the file picker, the Paste screenshots button as often as needed, or drag-and-drop. Nothing is processed until you select Recognize screenshots.
  4. Select Recognize screenshots, then review every included row’s port, matched item, and quantity. Only included, fully matched rows are applied; unresolved or missing values are not turned into zero.
  5. Use Export for a JSON backup. For Import, choose a file, read the preview, and only then confirm replacement. Inventory change history records exact before-and-after quantities; clearing history does not change inventory.
  6. Open Shared warehouses to send one warehouse or All warehouses from the selected profile. A received link first opens a read-only preview with each warehouse collapsed. Choose View without saving, Save in Shared with me, or Save and download Excel. Saved copies stay only in that browser; the workbook uses sorted Stock and Combined overview tables with the same English headings in every language so files from several people are easier to combine.

Profiles, stock, accepted shared snapshots, OCR work, and history stay in this browser. Screenshots are processed in memory and are not uploaded or saved. A complete share link can be opened and forwarded by anyone who receives it. Export personal inventory before clearing browser data or moving to another browser or device.

Build and execute a crafting projectChoose outputs, calculate materials, account for inventory, and create a collection-and-purchase plan.

Example: Plan two copies of a recipe

You want to prepare the materials for two ships.

  1. Open Crafting Projects and, in Choose build, select the recipe and set quantity to 2.
  2. In Materials, keep Track progress manually or explicitly choose Use saved warehouse stock, then review the requirements and any incomplete values.
  3. In Collect & deliver, review Owned, Collect, Buy and Planned cargo; open Supply & Cargo for the trip, then use Record work to track progress or partial completion.

What you get: You get an editable two-ship project with the inventory basis you chose and a clear next step.

  1. In Step 1, choose what to craft and its quantity. For a ship, this Naval Action ship builder creates crafting requirements and costs from the chosen blueprint, frame wood, and trim wood; it does not compare ship or wood stats.
  2. In Step 2, choose Per-item estimate or Whole craft batches. Then keep Manual inventory, or select Saved warehouse profile plus one warehouse or all warehouses. Select Use these settings and continue.
  3. In Step 3, review materials, costs, missing prices, and stock coverage. With manual inventory, enter what you already have. Saved warehouse stock is remote: covered quantities still become pickup tasks at their stored ports.
  4. Open the production-chain details to follow the final item through crafted ingredients to raw materials. Raw-material totals remain authoritative; whole-batch mode may show surplus, and a missing price is never counted as zero.
  5. In the crafting-project supply map, review the rounded route quantities and select Open supplies in shopping map. The current project is saved first; the handoff includes warehouse pickups and remaining NPC purchases.
  6. In Step 4, start the frozen execution checklist and confirm pickup, purchase, cargo, and crafting stages in order. Checklist confirmations track progress only; they do not change saved warehouse quantities.

Recent projects, named plans, and execution checklists are stored in this browser. An active checklist keeps its frozen original plan even if the workspace later changes.

Crafting Projects
Build and compare ships and cannonsBuild up to three ship configurations, compare A with B or C, and inspect up to three cannons.

Example: Compare two wood choices

You are deciding between two hull wood options for the same ship.

  1. Open the Configurator and create build A and build B for the same ship.
  2. Change the frame or planking wood in one build at a time, keeping the rest equal.
  3. Compare the displayed ship stats and warnings for the two builds.

What you get: You can see how each wood choice changes the configured ship stats.

  1. Open Ships & Cannons. Configuration A is your starting build and the reference for every difference.
  2. Choose a ship in A. Select a cannon for each deck, then add frame and planking woods, permanent upgrades, and knowledge/books when needed.
  3. Open Build actions to copy, clear, or move a configuration. Clear and replacement actions ask for confirmation before removing choices.
  4. Compare B or C with A. Read both the exact and percentage difference, or choose Show only differences. Higher and lower are not universally better or worse.
  5. Open Compare cannons, filter by gun family, ship, and deck, and choose up to three cannons.
  6. Read each compatibility result literally: Verified compatible, Verified incompatible, or Compatibility unknown. Unknown is never treated as compatible.
  7. Saving is optional. “Save builds in this browser” keeps a local copy on this device; “Delete saved builds from this browser” removes that copy. Your choices are not uploaded.

Ship-card values combine the healthy base ship with the selected frame and planking woods. This includes ship speed, handling, crew, repairs, hit points, thickness and additional wood effects. Rating also uses the selected cannons. Books, upgrades, trims, perks and cargo effects are excluded. Their raw modifiers are listed separately. Missing or outdated reference values remain unavailable.

Plan supplies, cargo, stops, and tripsBuild a shopping list directly or continue from a crafting-project handoff.

Example: Plan a pickup and cargo delivery

You want 100 Oak Logs from your warehouse and NPC stock.

  1. Open Supply & Cargo, add Oak Logs with quantity 100, and choose a cargo ship if you want capacity calculations.
  2. Select a saved warehouse profile to use its existing stock, then review coverage, remaining NPC purchases, cargo weight, and ordered stops.
  3. Select Show on Map V5 and use the stop list to plan the collection and delivery in game.

What you get: You get pickup and purchase stops with their quantities, plus any shortages that still need resolving.

  1. Add each product and quantity, and choose a cargo ship when you want capacity and trip calculations. A crafting handoff may already contain both warehouse pickups and NPC purchases.
  2. Choose one port rule: best prices near a starting port, best prices in one nation, or cheapest prices anywhere. Complete the required starting-port, distance, or nation fields.
  3. Review coverage, listed suppliers, base cost, port tax, cargo weight, ship capacity, and trips. Incomplete stock or missing weight stays visibly incomplete.
  4. Use the recommended pickup plan and open Pickup map when useful. A solid route means the quantities and ordered stop sequence are proven; a dashed route is only a suggestion.
  5. Save a list if you want to restore it later. ‘Mark this trip complete in this tab’ is only a page-session checkmark: it changes no list, route, warehouse quantity, or inventory and resets when the page session ends or the trip plan changes.

Relative distances and stop order are planning aids, not sailing directions or travel-time promises. Saved shopping lists remain in this browser on this device.

Supply & Cargo
Compare an NPC trade or look for routesEstimate a single buy-and-sell scenario or screen potential direct and regional routes.

Example: Test a trade budget

You want to screen a hypothetical purchase and sale before spending money.

  1. Choose a product, source port, consuming destination, and a quantity within your budget.
  2. Read the displayed source price, gross revenue, gross profit, return, and relative distance.
  3. Use the figures to decide whether the route deserves an in-game price and cost check.

What you get: You get a candidate trade calculation using the planner’s current evidence.

  1. For one scenario, choose a product, NPC source port, destination port listed by the game data as consuming that product, and quantity.
  2. Read the source price and stock, estimated reference sale price, gross revenue, gross profit, return, cargo weight, and relative distance. Unavailable evidence remains unavailable.
  3. For route discovery, choose nation, result count, profit or return ranking, cargo capacity, and direct or regional routes, then select Find routes.
  4. Optional ETA calibration uses a direct source–destination trip time you observed for a selected vessel. It lasts only for this page session, scales relative coordinate distance, and never changes profit ranking.

Unproven status: the planner works and produces route candidates, but independent traders have not yet confirmed their validity. Test every route in game and report the result. Destination prices remain unofficial estimates; profit excludes taxes, fees, sailing costs, and risk.

Trade Planner
Record a comparable port-to-port tripCreate one private JSON file using the same timing, compass, and cargo terms other players see.

Example: Record actual trip times

You want one comparable direct trip record for your chosen ship.

  1. Open Record a trip, start when Open World is visible and the ship can move, and stop when Enter Port is available.
  2. Enter both UTC times plus ports, ship, direct or interrupted status, fleet status, speed, compass, wind, and cargo load.
  3. Download one JSON file for this leg and keep it with your own notes.

What you get: You get a private, comparable trip file; uncertainty or a detour stays marked honestly.

  1. Open Record a trip. Start the timer when Open World is visible and your ship can move; stop when the destination’s Enter Port action becomes available. Enter both times in UTC.
  2. Choose the departure port, destination, ship, whether the leg was direct and uninterrupted, and whether you sailed alone or with fleet ships. The progress bar shows when all seven required fields are complete. Info-panel speed is optional; leave it blank if unsure.
  3. For optional compass readings, copy the game’s compact notation: W-1 is one tick left of W, W is exactly West, and W+1 is one tick right. For wind, record where the blue line starts rather than where its arrow points.
  4. For Cargo load, choose Empty, Max 65% hold filled, More than 65% hold filled, or Unknown. These are broad observation categories; departure and arrival times remain the primary evidence.
  5. Select Download trip file. Nothing is uploaded or stored by the site; send the downloaded JSON file through your usual channel. Use one file for exactly one port-to-port leg.

A detour, stop, battle, disconnect, fleet, or uncertain detail does not make a log useless. Record it honestly; the file marks whether it is eligible for the direct-solo baseline.

Understand updates, prices, and privacyWhat the figures mean, why contracts are excluded, and how each update is checked.

Example: Read a hypothetical tax example

A listed unit price is 10 Reals and you consider 25 units; these numbers are illustrative only.

  1. Read the listed unit price as 25 × 10 = 250 Reals before tax.
  2. For an illustrative 10% port tax, calculate 250 × 10% = 25 Reals and 250 + 25 = 275 Reals total; the game rounds transaction tax down.
  3. Check the exact total in game because item-to-port allocation, tax, stock, and update date all matter.

What you get: You understand the difference between unit price, transaction tax, and the separate sell direction.

Which price should I use?

Listed unit price (before tax) comes directly from the shop listing. Port tax is charged on the full quantity for one item at one port, and the game rounds that transaction tax down. An exact final total needs both the quantity and the item-to-port allocation. Port shop pays you remains the separate sell direction.

Player contracts are left out

The public numbers use only game-run port shops. Player contracts stay in the private source files and are never mixed into these prices. A missing price stays missing; it is not changed to zero or guessed.

When a new market day starts

A new market day starts at 10:00 UTC, after Naval Action’s daily maintenance. We publish only when all three game-data files are newer than that point, and we save one checked update per day.
